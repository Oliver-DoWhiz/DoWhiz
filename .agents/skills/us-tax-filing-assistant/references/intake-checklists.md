# Intake Checklists

Ask only for missing information. Start with classification, then collect route-specific documents.

## How to ask

- Extract facts already in the thread first. Do not ask again for information or files the user already gave you.
- If the route is unclear, ask only the minimum classification pack first.
- If the route is clear, send one concise route-specific checklist that covers only the missing facts and files.
- Do not ask the account question until the route and missing document list are mostly mapped, unless the user already volunteered account status.

## Universal intake fields

Collect these for every case before you drill deeper:

- tax year or years
- individual or business entity
- state of residence, work, or registration
- whether the user wants intake only, account creation, filing help, or all three
- any account status the user already volunteered

## Classification questions for an unclear individual case

Ask only enough to classify the filer:

1. Are you a U.S. citizen, a green card holder, or neither?
2. What visa or immigration status did you hold during the tax year?
3. On what dates were you physically present in the U.S. during the tax year and the two prior years?
4. Do you have an I-94 record or travel history you can upload?
5. Did you receive U.S. wages, scholarship income, fellowship income, contract income, or no U.S. income at all?
6. Which states did you live in or work in?

If classification is still unclear after those questions, stop and consult [escalation-cases.md](escalation-cases.md).

If the user already clearly said they are a U.S. citizen or green card holder and the rest of the facts are consistent, do not repeat the whole classification pack. Move straight to the route-specific checklist.

## NRA route checklist

Use this checklist when the filer is a nonresident alien or probably a nonresident alien.

### Required facts

- full legal name
- date of birth
- country of citizenship
- current visa or immigration status
- SSN or ITIN status
- U.S. address and foreign permanent address, if relevant
- all U.S. arrival and departure dates for the tax year and prior two years
- states where the user lived, studied, or worked
- school, employer, fellowship, or payer names
- exact place where the user worked in the U.S.

### Required files to request

- I-94 record or equivalent CBP travel history
- W-2 PDF or PDFs
- 1042-S PDF or PDFs
- 1099 PDF or PDFs
- prior-year 1040-NR or 8843, if available

### Additional files to ask for when relevant

- 1098-T
- pay stubs if a W-2 is missing
- treaty-related forms or employer communications
- passport biographic page
- I-20 or DS-2019 when exempt-individual or student or scholar questions matter

### Clarifying questions

- Did you have any U.S. income, or are you filing an 8843-only case?
- Did you have scholarship, fellowship, stipend, or treaty-exempt income?
- Did you do self-employment, contract work, freelancing, or gig work?
- Did you change visa category during the year?
- Did you previously file as an NRA or resident alien?

### Ready-to-route rule

Do not send the case to Sprintax until you have:

- a stable NRA classification or a clearly documented reason the case still appears NRA,
- the core income forms or a list of which ones are missing,
- the travel history or a clear note that it is still missing,
- the state list,
- the work or school details,
- the account decision.

## U.S. citizen or resident alien checklist

Use this checklist for Form 1040-style individual cases.

### Required facts

- full legal name
- SSN or ITIN
- filing status
- spouse information if relevant
- dependent information if relevant
- home state and work state
- whether the user had self-employment or side income

### Typical files to request

- W-2 PDFs
- 1099 PDFs
- 1098 forms if relevant
- prior-year return, if available
- brokerage, bank, mortgage, tuition, childcare, or marketplace insurance forms when relevant

### Clarifying questions

- Did you live in more than one state?
- Did you have self-employment or side-gig income?
- Did you receive marketplace insurance Form 1095-A?
- Are there dependents, education credits, mortgage interest, or major deductions to consider?

### Ready-to-route rule

Do not move to TurboTax until you know:

- the filing status,
- the state footprint,
- the main income document set,
- whether the user is an employee-only case or a more complex return,
- the account decision.

## Business checklist

Use this checklist for any company, partnership, corporation, multi-member LLC, or business-owner filing request.

### First questions

1. What is the entity type?
2. Is this actually a personal return with Schedule C, or a separate entity return?
3. Is it domestic or foreign?
4. Which return do you think applies: Schedule C, 1065, 1120-S, 1120, 1041, or something else?
5. Do you have an EIN?
6. Which states is the entity registered or operating in?

### Required facts

- legal entity name
- EIN
- entity type
- domestic or foreign status
- tax year
- state of formation and states of activity
- owners, partners, or shareholders at a high level
- whether there were foreign owners, foreign related parties, or foreign accounts

### Required business records

- prior-year return, if available
- bookkeeping export, P&L, and balance sheet
- payroll and contractor records
- K-1s, if applicable
- major fixed-asset or depreciation records if relevant

### Immediate escalation triggers

- foreign-owned disregarded entity
- foreign partner or foreign shareholder reporting
- Form 5472, 1120-F, 5471, 8858, or other international information returns
- uncertainty about whether the case is a business return or a Schedule C on an individual return

Only proceed to TurboTax business flows if the case stays within routine U.S. product scope and you have the account decision.
