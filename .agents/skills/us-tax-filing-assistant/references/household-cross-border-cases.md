# Household and Cross-Border Cases

Read this file when the case involves a spouse, couple, foreign payroll, foreign wages, foreign tax paid, foreign accounts, or a move into or out of the United States.

## Scenario families that need special handling

Treat these as first-class workflows, not edge cases:

- married couple where the spouses have different tax residency outcomes
- year-of-arrival or year-of-departure cases
- dual-status or first-year-choice cases
- one spouse with U.S. wages and the other spouse with foreign wages
- wages paid by a foreign employer for services performed partly inside and partly outside the United States
- nonresident spouse election or dual-status spouse election questions
- foreign tax paid that may require foreign tax credit or treaty review
- foreign bank, brokerage, retirement, or payroll accounts that may trigger Form 8938 or FBAR analysis
- community property state issues involving separate filing or an NRA spouse

## Minimum workflow for any cross-border household

1. Build a person-by-person matrix.
2. Build an income-stream-by-income-stream matrix.
3. Decide whether the issue is classification, sourcing, filing-status election, foreign-credit issue, foreign-account reporting, or a combination.
4. Route only after those matrices are stable.

## Person-by-person matrix

For each spouse or household member who affects the return, collect:

- citizenship
- green card status
- visa or immigration status during the tax year
- exact U.S. arrival and departure dates for the tax year and prior two years
- whether the person may be a resident alien, nonresident alien, dual-status individual, or first-year-choice case
- SSN or ITIN status
- state residence and work footprint

## Income-stream matrix

For each income stream, collect:

- who earned it
- employee versus contractor status
- payer legal name
- payer country and payroll country
- where the services were physically performed
- exact service dates or workday allocation
- document type: W-2, 1099, 1042-S, foreign wage statement, pay slips, or other payroll record
- U.S. withholding, foreign withholding, and social-insurance withholding
- whether foreign tax was paid or accrued
- whether the income includes bonus, equity, deferred compensation, or reimbursement items

## Household filing-status questions to resolve

Collect these before selecting Sprintax or TurboTax:

- Were the spouses married on December 31 of the tax year?
- Is one spouse a nonresident, dual-status individual, or first-year-choice case?
- Is a joint return election being considered for a nonresident or dual-status spouse?
- Did the household live in a community property state?
- Does either spouse have foreign accounts, foreign trusts, or foreign entities?

## Example: couple moved to the U.S., husband is paid from China, wife is paid from the U.S.

Use this exact intake order.

### 1. Classify each spouse separately

Collect for both husband and wife:

- citizenship and green card status
- visa or immigration status during the year
- exact U.S. arrival date and any later departures
- U.S. presence during the prior two years
- SSN or ITIN status

Do not assume they have the same tax status just because they moved together.

### 2. Build the move timeline

Collect:

- date they first entered the U.S. to live or work
- whether either spouse was ever in the U.S. earlier in the year
- states they lived in after the move
- whether they had a home or lease in another country after the move

### 3. Collect the husband's China-payroll facts

Collect:

- whether he is an employee or contractor
- employer legal name and country
- payroll country and bank account receiving the wages
- exact dates he performed services in China versus inside the United States
- foreign wage statements, pay slips, and any Chinese tax statements
- whether any U.S. withholding or reporting form exists
- whether Social Security, Medicare, or foreign social insurance was withheld

### 4. Collect the wife's U.S.-payroll facts

Collect:

- W-2 or other U.S. forms
- employer state and work state
- start date and any prior foreign work for the same employer
- federal and state withholding
- whether there are benefits, stock compensation, or dependent-care items

### 5. Collect foreign-account and foreign-tax facts for the household

Collect:

- foreign bank, brokerage, retirement, payroll, or payment-platform accounts
- maximum balances during the year if available
- whether foreign tax was paid on any compensation
- whether either spouse owned foreign entities or had signature authority

### 6. Map the possible case outcomes

Common branches to research are:

- both spouses remain NRA for the year
- one or both spouses become dual-status
- one spouse qualifies for first-year choice
- one spouse is resident or citizen and the other is a nonresident spouse election candidate
- both spouses are resident aliens and must report worldwide income

If the route depends on dual-status treatment, first-year choice, a nonresident-spouse election, foreign tax credit treatment, or community property, pause and verify the controlling IRS source before filing.

## Practical rule

If a foreign employer keeps paying someone after they start performing services in the United States, never rely on payer country alone. You must collect where the services were actually performed, what tax was withheld, and whether the household may need resident-return, dual-status, treaty, foreign-tax-credit, payroll-tax, or foreign-account reporting analysis.
